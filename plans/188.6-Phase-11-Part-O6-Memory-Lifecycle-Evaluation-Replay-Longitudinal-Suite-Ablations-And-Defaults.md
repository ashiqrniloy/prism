# Phase 11 Part O.6 — memory lifecycle evaluation: replay harness, longitudinal multi-project suite, ablations, and defaults

## Objectives

- Measure the memory lifecycle of plans 188.1–188.5, and decide every provisional default with paired, clustered statistics. This includes:
  - plan 188.1 Task 10's rerun, retrieval ablations, and lexical floor calibration (moved here on 2026-10-19)
  - the promotion gate, retention thresholds, capacity, and delivery tiers
- **Two levels:**
  - **Component level, cheap and deterministic.**
    - Record OM ledgers once: synthetic ones, and dogfood sessions with consent.
    - Replay promotion and retention policies offline against gold labels.
    - Metrics: promotion precision and recall, scope accuracy, type accuracy, duplicate rate, **archive regret**, **graveyard index**, and the active-set growth curve.
    - Extractor outputs are cached by content hash, so twenty policy variants cost almost nothing.
  - **End-to-end.** A longitudinal, multi-project suite with a simulated user.
    - One persona works across K projects and N sessions, with planted, labelled facts.
    - Metrics: task success, cross-project leakage, stale-use rate, repeated-error rate, recall at the right time, abstention correctness, tokens per session, and poison persistence.
- **Public benchmarks:**
  - LongMemEval-S: knowledge-update, temporal, and abstention categories.
  - MemoryAgentBench conflict resolution.
  - DreamBench-SWE and VibeMemBench where licences allow; both are coding-specific with executable oracles.
  - LoCoMo for comparison with other systems only.
- **Statistics:**
  - errors clustered by persona and project
  - effect sizes with confidence intervals
  - Holm correction across each ablation family
  - judge panels for gold labels, with a human-audited sample

**Evidence and constraints** (owner-approved memory-lifecycle analysis, 2026-10-19):

- **Hygiene metrics.** DreamBench-SWE (arXiv 2608.20664) measures memory hygiene with executable oracles and reports a stale-use rate of 0.20 and a repeated-error rate of 0.57. Its confirmatory hybrid-vs-verbatim result was not significant, which shows why paired, powered, preregistered comparisons matter.
- **Record form matters, and comparability is contested.** VibeMemBench (arXiv 2609.23570) found that record form, not retrieval, decides whether memory helps a coding agent. Its arm comparability is disputed.
- **Evaluation rigour.** Plans 197–201 follow Kapoor et al. 2024 (arXiv 2407.01502), Zhu et al. 2025 (arXiv 2507.02825), and Dietterich 1998 (paired tests).
- **Sessions are correlated within a persona and project.** Treating them as independent overstates significance, so errors are clustered.
- **The evals plans run after 189 on the release path** (197–201: clustered errors, judge panels, simulated users, durable stores). This plan cannot depend on them. It builds what it needs inside `scripts/memory-bench` (plan 188.2 Task 1 (i)), and Further Actions records the port to `@arnilo/prism-evals` once 198–200 land.
